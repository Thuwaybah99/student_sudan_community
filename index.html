THE BEGINNING

When something needed doing, I wanted to help.

I didn't begin university thinking about leadership titles. I began by noticing where people needed support.

In my electronic engineering department, information didn't always reach everyone easily. Schedules, course details, academic updates, and communication with professors sometimes needed someone willing to follow up, organize, and share what they learned.

At the same time, difficult engineering courses could feel overwhelming — especially for classmates balancing work, study, and the uncertainty created by the war in Sudan. I discovered that explaining complex ideas in simpler ways was something I genuinely enjoyed and could offer.

Over the years, these two ways of helping became central to my university experience: being a reliable point of contact and making learning more accessible.

01 / STUDENT REPRESENTATION

Leadership is often the work between the lines.

Listening, coordinating, following through — and making sure people are not left out of the conversation.

Together with my peers, at our appreciation gathering.

I served on a student committee selected from our batch to help bridge communication between students and university staff.

01

Connecting people

Communicated with professors and relevant university departments, then relayed important information back to our classmates.

02

Making information accessible

Helped gather and distribute course-related information, updates, and academic materials so students could stay informed.

03

Helping things run smoothly

Supported coordination and scheduling when needed, following up so that information did not stop at the administration level.

“I learned that leadership can be as simple — and as important — as making sure everybody gets the information they need.”
02 / PEER EDUCATION

Make it clearer.
Pass it forward.

What began as my own way of understanding engineering concepts became a resource other students could return to, share, and build on.

✎01 / UNDERSTAND

Find the simplest explanation

I enjoy breaking down advanced mathematics and difficult engineering topics into smaller, clearer ideas — the kind I wished were always available when studying.

▤02 / DOCUMENT

Keep useful notes and videos

I saved my explanations as organized study notes and recorded lessons, so they were not limited to a single conversation or one exam season.

↗03 / SHARE

Let the knowledge travel

Classmates used and shared my materials, and students in later batches found them when they reached the same courses.

✳
WHY THIS MATTERED

Learning during the war in Sudan

Our studies were disrupted by war, displacement, uneven internet access, and the pressure of work alongside university. Some courses were delivered online. Not every student had the connectivity or time to search for explanations across many sources.

My notes and recorded explanations offered one more way to study: material already collected, simplified, and shared by someone taking the same courses. Students told me that this helped them prepare for exams and continue into their next semesters.

03 / THE IMPACT

The part that stays with me is the people.

After exams, classmates would message me to say a note or a video had helped them finally understand a topic, perform better, or pass.

Sometimes, students from batches below mine would recognize me on campus because my materials had already reached them. Their appreciation meant a great deal to me — not because it made me known, but because it showed how far a shared explanation could go.

Those conversations are among my most meaningful university memories. They reminded me that the value of a resource is not just in creating it; it is in the doors it helps someone else open.

A small act of sharing can travel further than we expect. ✳

LOOKING FORWARD

What I carry into the future.

My university experience taught me that leadership is not only about making decisions. It is also about showing up consistently, sharing what you know, and helping people move forward together — especially when circumstances make that difficult.

As I pursue further study, I hope to carry this same commitment into new communities: learning deeply, explaining generously, and making space for others to succeed.
